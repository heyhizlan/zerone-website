01

ADFL

Active Damping Force Levelling — an automatic variable valve inside the damper.

SEC-01 / ADFL

The damper keeps adjusting after you stop.

Damping force is what a damper does: it resists the movement of the suspension by forcing oil through a restriction. Make the restriction smaller and the force goes up. Make it larger and the force comes down. Everything else in a damper exists to hold that restriction in the right place.

ADFL varies the restriction on its own. It is an automatic variable valve: it takes its signal from the hydraulic oil pressure already inside the damper, and varies the valve against it — increasing or reducing damping force according to the road surface and the way the car is being driven. There is no sensor, no control unit and nothing for the driver to operate. The pressure is the signal.

Because the valve responds to pressure rather than to a chosen setting, it works across the whole damping force range instead of at one point in it. Steering response, roadability and traction hold up when the surface changes under the car — which, on the roads this was developed for, it does constantly.

Engineering ADFL was engineered by Mr. Kichiro, technical engineer, Japan, and tuned for Malaysian road conditions. It is fitted to the SSR700 and SSR700 PLUS.

02

Mono-tube & twin-tube

Two constructions run through the range. Neither is a better version of the other — they behave differently.

SEC-02 / CONSTRUCTION

Everything a damper does happens because oil is pushed through a restriction. How many tubes that oil lives in, and where the gas sits, decides how the damper behaves when it is worked hard, and how it behaves when it is barely working at all.

Mono-tube

SSR700 · SSR700 PLUS

One pressure tube. The piston works directly in it, and a floating piston further down divides the oil from a pressurised gas charge. Oil, gas and piston all share a single bore, so nothing is nested inside anything else.

  • Heat leaves quickly. The working oil sits against the outer wall, so it can shed heat straight into the airstream. Damping force stays consistent through sustained work.
  • Larger piston for the same outside diameter. More area means the same force is made at lower pressure, and lower pressure is easier on the oil and the seals.
  • Firmer at low piston speed. It reports the surface honestly. On a broken road that is information; some drivers want less of it.
  • One tube is the structure. There is no outer casing protecting the bore, so it is the more exposed of the two.

Twin-tube

SSR550 · SSR500 PLUS

An inner working tube, where the piston runs, inside an outer reserve tube. A base valve at the foot of the inner tube passes oil out into the reserve as the rod enters, and draws it back as the rod leaves. The gas cushion sits in the reserve, not against the piston.

  • More compliant over small inputs. Two valves share the work — the piston and the base valve — and the damper can be softer at low speed without giving up control higher up.
  • Easier to live with daily. Expansion joints, patched tarmac and speed humps arrive with less of an edge.
  • Heat has further to travel. It crosses the reserve before it reaches outside air, so sustained hard work heats the oil sooner than in a mono-tube.
  • The outer tube protects the inner one. The bore the piston runs in is not the surface exposed to the road.
Across the range

Mono-tube: SSR700, SSR700 PLUS — the construction ADFL is fitted to, for aggressive handling on street and track. · Twin-tube: SSR550, SSR500 PLUS — for daily driving and occasional track use. Choose by what the car has to do most days, not by which sounds more serious.

03

Three axes of adjustment

What a ZerOne kit actually lets you change, and what each one costs you elsewhere.

SEC-03 / ADJUSTMENT
01

Ride height Threaded collars

The damper body is threaded, and a pair of locking collars runs on that thread carrying the spring seat. Wind the collars and the spring seat moves; the car settles to a new static height. A second collar locks against the first so the setting cannot walk under load.

Height set this way is real, but it is not free. Moving the spring seat changes where the piston sits in the tube at rest — and that is what the next axis exists to solve.

02

Body-shift Height without losing stroke

This is the part most people get wrong, so it is worth being exact about. A damper has a fixed amount of travel. Lower the car by winding the spring seat down and the rod is already further into the tube before you have driven anywhere — you have taken your new ride height out of the compression travel you had left. The bump stop is now closer than it was, and on a bad surface the suspension reaches it sooner. The car sits lower and rides worse.

Body-shift changes something else. The damper body is repositioned within its lower mount, which shortens the assembly as a whole. The car comes down by exactly that amount — but the spring seat and the piston have not moved relative to the tube, so the rod starts from where it always did. Ride height drops. Travel does not.

The SSR700, SSR700 PLUS and SSR550 are all Hi/Lo height adjustable with body-shift, so the two can be set independently: stance from one, stroke from the other.

03

Damping force Discrete, repeatable clicks

The adjuster changes the restriction the oil meets, and it does so in counted steps rather than on a continuous dial. That matters more than it sounds: a click is a position you can find again. You can go two clicks firmer for a session, put it back afterwards, and know the car is where it was.

SSR700 · SSR700 PLUS 32 steps Compression and rebound on separate adjusters, so the two ends of the stroke are set independently.
SSR550 24 steps A single damping adjustment across the range, for daily driving and occasional track use.
SSR500 PLUS Rebound & compression Adjustable to driver preference, vehicle setup, track and external conditions.

On the SSR700 and SSR700 PLUS the ADFL valve keeps working underneath whichever click you have chosen — the setting sets the range, the valve manages what happens inside it.

SAE9254

A silicon-chromium spring steel. A suspension spring is asked to deflect several million times and return to the same free length every time; alloys in this family are specified where fatigue strength and resistance to sag matter more than outright hardness. A spring that has settled has quietly changed your ride height and your corner weights.

Cold-coiled, CNC-wound

The wire is formed cold and wound under CNC control, which holds the geometry — coil pitch, free length, end squareness — to a tighter tolerance than hot forming. On the car that means the rate you specified is the rate you get, and the same rate on both sides.

Anti-Corrosion Coating

Surface corrosion on a spring is not cosmetic. A corrosion pit is a stress raiser, and fatigue cracks start at stress raisers. ACC is there to stop that beginning — which, in a climate that is wet for most of the year, is the difference between a spring that lasts and one that fails at the surface.

Piston & casing — SSR700

The SSR700 runs a larger piston diameter and a carbon thread casing. More piston area makes a given damping force at lower working pressure, which is easier on the oil and on the seals; the thread casing is where the height collars run, so it is the part that has to stay true and stay adjustable after a few Malaysian monsoons.

Setting the range

Adjustment is only worth having if somebody sets it properly.

SEC-05 / SETUP

There is no correct setting.
There is a correct setting for your car.

Everything on this page describes a range. Where you sit inside it depends on things a website cannot know: how the weight is distributed in your car, what spring rate is on it, which tyre you run and at what pressure, the surface you drive on most, and how you want the car to answer the wheel.

That is the whole reason the kit adjusts. It is also why the last step is a conversation rather than a checkout. ZerOne dealers fit and set these kits every day across six states — they will tell you where to start, what to change when the car does not do what you want, and which kit is right for the car before you commit to one.
